### T-5070 · A full-length model shot cut by a frame edge matches no phenotype at all
**Status:** Ready | **Profile:** P4-critical-architecture
**Found by:** the [[T-4970]] third-pass accuracy scoring, 2026-08-05

A `body-visible = full` reading that also touches a frame edge satisfies neither the full-product nor
the partial on-model rules, so a real, common shot shape gets no phenotype at all. What started as a
34%-of-SPACINI29 on-model gap turned out, on a second dataset, to be a much larger packshot-side gap
with the same root cause — which changes which fix is likely correct.

**Problem(s):**
- **SPACINI29 on-model gap.** `body-visible` reads `full` on 29 of 86 SPACINI29 images.
  `front-on-model-full-product` / `back-on-model-full-product` accept `body-visible = full` but both
  hard-require `intersection-count = 0`; `front-on-model-partial` / `back-on-model-partial` accept an
  edge intersect but their `body-visible` `anyOf` lists only `three-quarter`, `half`, `bust` — not
  `full`. Ground truth (`test/datasets/SPACINI29/RAW IMAGES/dataset notes.md`, do-not-edit) says no
  image in this dataset is fully inside the frame: 16 touch one edge, 70 touch two. Cause: the taxonomy
  assumes a full-length subject implies a full-product shot. Effect: an image measured `full` that
  touches any edge satisfies neither branch. Consequence: those 29 images can never be assigned at any
  threshold — not a coverage problem a bar move can fix — and this is now the binding cap on phenotype
  coverage (33.7%), not the CLIP thresholds. It was invisible until [[T-4990]] closed a separate
  under-count bug (the detector used to misreport `intersection-count = 0` for 6 images, letting a few
  of these through the full-product gate on a detection error); with that gate honest, the gap is
  exposed.
- **The same gate is much bigger than the on-model case, measured on JBComplete (100 images,
  2026-08-05).** `intersection-count = 0` is a hard requirement of all five `*-packshot` phenotypes and
  both `*-on-model-full-product` phenotypes — 7 of the 18 phenotypes total. Only 27 of 100 JBComplete
  images measure `intersection-count = 0`, and only 25 of those also clear the `hero-is-human = FALSE` +
  `background-type = SOLIDCOLOR` gates, so 75% of a real catalogue set can reach no packshot phenotype
  at any threshold, before orientation is even consulted. On SPACINI29 it's worse — ground truth says no
  image is fully inside the frame at all, which is why only the two `*-on-model-partial` phenotypes have
  ever fired there. The `body-visible = full` + intersect case from the SPACINI29 problem above bites
  JBComplete too: 20 of 100 images, 17 of which get no phenotype — 17% here against 34% on SPACINI29,
  same defect, smaller share, because JBComplete is mostly product shots rather than full-length model
  shots.
- **`closeup-image` absorbs the overflow with no positive discriminator.** It is the mirror image of the
  same gate and behaves as a sink: its whole rule is `hero-is-human = FALSE` + `intersection-count >= 3`
  — nothing about coverage ratio, crop tightness, or subject scale. Every tightly-cropped product photo
  that the packshot rules just rejected for touching edges lands here instead. Measured precision on
  JBComplete: 0 correct out of 8 assigned — it absorbed real `front-packshot`, `back-on-model-full-product`,
  `interior-shot`, `diagonal-packshot`, and `lifestyle-hero` images. Whatever option is chosen for the
  two problems above, `closeup-image` needs a positive discriminator, not just "touches 3+ edges."

**Decision(s):**
- **Not taken here — a product call between three defensible shapes, reframed by the JBComplete
  findings above.** Two of the three options only address the on-model half of the problem and leave the
  packshot half (JBComplete gap, `closeup-image` sink) unaddressed; the third — questioning what the
  measurements mean — now looks stronger, because `intersection-count` is being used as a proxy for "the
  whole product is in shot," but on a catalogue image cropped to the product it's closer to a proxy for
  "the crop is tight," which is the opposite of what the packshot rules assume. The three shapes: (1)
  add `full` to the two partial rules' `body-visible` `anyOf` — treats "cut by an edge" as the
  discriminator, makes `full` mean only "how much of the body is framed"; (2) add a fourth on-model pair
  (`front-/back-on-model-full-length-cropped`) — keeps `partial` meaning a genuinely partial body, at the
  cost of two more phenotypes right after [[T-5040]] cut three; (3) treat `body-visible = full` +
  intersects as a measurement contradiction and revisit what `body-visible` means, since CLIP is calling
  a top-and-bottom-cropped model "full." Option 3 deserves weighing first: 29 of these images are cut at
  *both* top and bottom, and calling that a `full` body view may be the actual error. (open)

**Test(s):**
- Acceptance test: an edge-cropped full-length on-model shot receives a phenotype; the SPACINI29
  misassignment rate stays under M11's 5%; and whichever option is taken is written into
  `imagePhenotypes.md` with its reasoning. (not yet run)
