### T-5070 · A full-length model shot cut by a frame edge matches no phenotype at all
**Status:** Ready | **Profile:** P4-critical-architecture
**Found by:** the [[T-4970]] third-pass accuracy scoring, 2026-08-05

**34% of SPACINI29 falls in a hole between two rules.** `body-visible` reads `full` on **29 of 86**
images. Follow what that value can reach:

- `front-on-model-full-product` / `back-on-model-full-product` accept `body-visible = full`, but both
  hard-require `intersection-count = 0`.
- `front-on-model-partial` / `back-on-model-partial` accept an edge intersect, but their `body-visible`
  `anyOf` lists only `three-quarter`, `half`, `bust` — **not** `full`.

Ground truth (`dataset notes.md`, do-not-edit) says **no image in this dataset is fully inside the
frame**; 16 touch one edge, 70 touch two. **Cause:** the taxonomy assumes a full-length subject implies
a full-product shot. **Effect:** an image measured `full` that touches any edge satisfies neither
branch. **Consequence:** 29 images can never be assigned at *any* threshold — this is not a coverage
problem a bar move can fix, and it is now the binding cap on phenotype coverage (33.7%) rather than the
CLIP thresholds.

**This was invisible until now** because [[T-4990]] closed the under-count: the detector used to report
`intersection-count = 0` for 6 images, which let a few of these through the full-product gate on a
detection error. With zero under-counts, the gate is honest and the gap is exposed.

---

**The hole is much bigger than `body-visible` (measured on JBComplete, 100 images, 2026-08-05).**
The `body-visible = full` framing above is one instance of a general rule: **`intersection-count = 0`
is a hard requirement of all five `*-packshot` phenotypes and both `*-on-model-full-product`
phenotypes** — 7 of the 18. Only **27 of 100** JBComplete images measure `intersection-count = 0`, and
only 25 of those also clear the `hero-is-human = FALSE` + `background-type = SOLIDCOLOR` gates. So
**75% of a real catalogue set can reach no packshot phenotype at any threshold**, before orientation
is even consulted. On SPACINI29 it is worse: ground truth says *no* image is fully inside the frame,
which is why only the two `*-on-model-partial` phenotypes have ever fired there.

The `body-visible = full` + intersect case bites JBComplete too: 20 of 100 images, 17 of which get no
phenotype. That is 17% here against 34% on SPACINI29 — same defect, smaller share, because JBComplete
is mostly product shots rather than full-length model shots.

**`closeup-image` is the mirror image of the same gate and behaves as a sink.** Its whole rule is
`hero-is-human = FALSE` + `intersection-count >= 3`. Nothing about coverage ratio, crop tightness or
subject scale. So every tightly-cropped product photo the packshot rules just rejected for touching
edges lands here instead. Measured precision on JBComplete: **0 correct out of 8 assigned** — it
absorbed real `front-packshot`, `back-on-model-full-product`, `interior-shot`, `diagonal-packshot` and
`lifestyle-hero` images. Whatever option is chosen above, `closeup-image` needs a positive
discriminator, not just "touches 3+ edges".

**This reframes the decision.** Options 1 and 2 in the list above only address the on-model half.
The packshot half needs its own answer, and option 3 (question what the measurements mean) now looks
stronger: `intersection-count` is being used as a proxy for *"the whole product is in shot"*, but on a
catalogue image cropped to the product it is closer to a proxy for *"the crop is tight"* — which is the
opposite of what the packshot rules assume.

**The decision is a product call and is not taken here.** Three shapes, all defensible:
1. Add `full` to the two partial rules' `body-visible` `anyOf` — treats "cut by an edge" as the
   discriminator and makes `full` mean only "how much of the body is framed".
2. Add a fourth on-model pair (`front-/back-on-model-full-length-cropped`) — keeps `partial` meaning a
   genuinely partial body, at the cost of two more phenotypes right after [[T-5040]] cut three.
3. Treat `body-visible = full` + intersects as a measurement contradiction and revisit what
   `body-visible` means, since CLIP is calling a top-and-bottom-cropped model "full".

Option 3 deserves weighing first: 29 of these images are cut at *both* top and bottom, and calling
that a `full` body view may be the actual error.

**Acceptance:** an edge-cropped full-length on-model shot receives a phenotype; the SPACINI29
misassignment rate stays under M11's 5%; and whichever option is taken is written into
`imagePhenotypes.md` with its reasoning.
