### T-6900 · SMASHEDLEMON45 hang is upstream of the matching waterfall, not SiblingPropagator
**Status:** Ready | **Profile:** P1-feature-worker
**Found by:** [[T-5200]] verification session, 2026-08-11. Built on evidence from [[T-5200]] and
[[T-5210]] — recorded here because it corrects both tickets' root-cause assumption.

**The problem.** T-5200 shipped a real, tested fix for `SiblingPropagator`'s O(unmatched × matched)
loose-relation scan (duplicate profile-build removed, inverted token index added, `crossBracketCandidates`
threaded through as a further filter — see T-5200's 2026-08-11 update for the full verification log).
Rerunning `X SMASHEDLEMON45` (1774 images, match-only) against the fixed code **still hangs** — timed out
twice (10 min, then 8 min) with no improvement over the original 45-minute baseline.

**What the fix's own instrumentation proved.** Temporary per-bracket `Stopwatch` logging was added to
`ImageMatcher.RunWaterfall` (every bracket from `FolderNameEnrichment` through `SiblingPropagator` through
`ApplyConvergenceBonus`) and exercised on two control jobs — a 5-image slice of real SMASHEDLEMON45 images,
and a 5-image synthetic set with fabricated family IDs guaranteed absent from the real Excel catalogue
(deliberately 100% unmatchable). Both control jobs logged every bracket's timing correctly and completed
in under 25 seconds total, confirming the instrumentation itself works and is fast at small scale.

The same instrumentation, run against the full 1774-image SMASHEDLEMON45 job, **produced zero log lines
for any bracket — not even Bracket 1** — after 8+ minutes, while `Prism.Api.exe` burned ~3 sustained CPU
cores the entire time (measured: 46.5s CPU consumed in a 15s wall-clock sample, `Get-Process` delta). This
is the same live CPU/GC signature originally used ([[T-5200]], 2026-08-10/11) to diagnose "the" hang — but
it now proves definitively that `ImageMatcher.RunWaterfall` is never entered during it. **The bottleneck is
entirely upstream, in `MatchingService.cs`'s pre-match pipeline**, between image ingest and
`ImageMatcher.Run` at `MatchingService.cs:144`.

**What's upstream, unmeasured, and now the two leading candidates:**
1. **Per-image feature-analysis loop** (`MatchingService.cs:79-125`) — runs `Parallel.For` decode + hash +
   `FeatureAnalysisService.EvaluateCandidates` per image in `ClipChunkSize` chunks, even with
   `SkipClassification=true` (classification itself is skipped via the `doClassify` gate, but decode/hash/
   feature-analysis are not). Untested at 1774-image scale for cost.
2. **`Deduplicate`/`FindDuplicates`** (`MatchingService.cs:138-140` → `MatchingService.cs:339-371`, backed
   by `classification.FindDuplicates(hashEntries)`) — hash-grouping across all images; shape/complexity of
   `FindDuplicates` itself was not read this session and needs verification (could be O(n) grouping, or
   something worse — unconfirmed either way).

Neither was profiled directly this session — the investigation stopped at "waterfall never entered" per
scope boundary (this session's `/pair` was scoped to `SiblingPropagator.cs` + `ImageMatcher.cs` only; the
pre-match pipeline is `MatchingService.cs`, explicitly out of bounds without a fresh scope confirmation).

**A separate, real finding surfaced along the way — the token-discard question.** Hand-tracing
`SiblingPropagator.BuildProfile("26000_775-725_1_B2C.jpg")` through its actual regex patterns:
`TokenSplitPattern` splits into `[26000, 775, 725, 1, b2c]`. `"1"` is discarded (`part.Length < 2`).
`"775"` and `"725"` are discarded by `ShotSuffixPattern`'s `\d{1,3}` branch — a blanket rule for shot
numbers that also catches any 1-3 digit token, including what may be a real product/color/variant code in
this dataset's naming convention. `"b2c"` splits at letter-digit boundaries into `b`/`2`/`c`, all
length-1, all discarded. Only `"26000"` survives — the whole 1774-image batch likely collapses to a
handful of near-degenerate single-token profiles keyed on the leading numeric ID alone. **This is a real,
separate defect candidate** (SiblingPropagator's relation logic can't discriminate on profiles this thin),
but it is **not confirmed as this hang's cause**: a 5-image synthetic control set built specifically to be
100%-unmatchable (thin/degenerate profiles included) did not hang. Whether `775`/`725`-style short numeric
codes are being wrongly discarded is a legitimate question worth its own investigation, but decoupled from
the volume-triggered hang this ticket tracks — the two may or may not turn out to share a cause.
