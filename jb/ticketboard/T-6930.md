### T-6930 · Pass 1's chunk barrier caps effective parallelism at ~3 of 8 threads
**Status:** Ready | **Profile:** P1-feature-worker
**Found by:** [[T-6910]], 2026-08-11 — third finding, split out on close so it isn't lost in the archive.

`MatchingService`'s `Analyze` loop (`MatchingService.cs`, chunked `Parallel.For` around line ~79) chunks
at `ClipChunkSize = 8` and runs `Parallel.For` *within* each chunk, so every 8 images is a synchronization
barrier. Live measurement on a running SMASHEDLEMON45 job (`Get-Process Prism.Api`, CPU delta over wall
clock) showed a **sustained 3.0 cores**, not the 8 `MaxDegreeOfParallelism` allows.

**Cause.** The dataset mixes 1.4 MP and 18.8 MP images whose per-image analyze cost differs **13×**
(422 ms vs 5514 ms). Seven threads sit idle waiting for the one 18.8 MP image in their chunk before the
next chunk's barrier releases.

**Why this matters beyond one dataset.** Documented batch cap is 10,000 images (`Input.Images.amount.max`).
On a batch with this kind of size variance, this is a multi-hour job dominated by idle cores, not compute.

**Shape of the fix (from T-6910, not decided further here):** either a flat `Parallel.ForEach` over all
images with chunking applied only to the CLIP batch call, or size-aware chunk packing (bin images by
estimated cost rather than arrival order). Note the chunk size exists to bound how many decoded images
are held in memory for the batched CLIP `Run` — any restructure has to preserve that bound (8 × 18.8 MP ×
4 bytes ≈ 600 MB today). This is a design choice, not settled here.

**Acceptance:** measure sustained core utilization on the same SMASHEDLEMON45 job before/after; report
the wall-clock change; confirm the CLIP batch memory bound is preserved; no CiMini golden regression.

**Files:** `jb/src/core/Services/Matching/MatchingService.cs` (`PrepareLambda`/chunked loop, `ClipChunkSize`).
