### T-5040 · Prune the phenotype set to the ones PRISM actually needs
**Status:** Ready | **Profile:** P4-critical-architecture

The 21 phenotypes in `ImageRoles.json` were authored by an agent, not derived from what PRISM has to
decide. At least one of them — `on-model-with-accessories` — has no identified consumer. There are
probably others. Every phenotype that exists but serves nothing still costs: a rule that can fire and
steal an image from a phenotype that does matter, a slot in the precedence order, and a line in every
coverage measurement that makes the numbers look worse than they are.

**The job is not "delete the bad ones".** It is to derive the set from the constraints, using the
existing feature→phenotype mapping model, and treat it as a constraint-satisfaction problem (CP-SAT
shaped, not necessarily a CP-SAT solver). Expect many iterations.

**Hard constraint, accepted by the user 04/08/26:** a phenotype never encodes the product type. A polo
and a t-shirt are distinct product types — that distinction belongs to the matcher — but both resolve
to the same phenotype. Phenotype describes view and composition only. Product type re-enters one step
later, in `DetOrderRules.json`, which maps phenotype → det slot *per product type*. Any candidate
phenotype that only one product type could satisfy is malformed and must be rejected by the model.

**Constraints to encode, at minimum:**
- Every phenotype must be reachable — some assignment of real feature values satisfies its `required`
  block. A rule no image can satisfy is dead.
- Every phenotype must be *distinguishable* — no two phenotypes may have `required` blocks where one
  is a subset of the other, unless keeping both is justified (see below).
- Every phenotype must be consumed — some `DetOrderRules` product type asks for it in a det slot.
  This is the constraint `on-model-with-accessories` appears to fail.
- Every det slot in `DetOrderRules.json` must be reachable from at least one phenotype.

**Two collisions already measured** (`test/datasets/JBComplete/README.md` §3):

1. `ghost-*` and `*-packshot` have identical `required` blocks except that ghost *also* accepts
   `clipping-path = true` alongside `background-type = SOLIDCOLOR`. Any garment on a solid background
   satisfies both, and `front-packshot` (index 7) wins over `ghost-front` (index 13) on list order.
2. `on-model-with-accessories` sits after `front-on-model-partial` and all three scarf images in
   `3 images.zip` satisfy both, so the earlier rule always wins.

**Rule for keeping a collision:** an unresolved collision is acceptable *only* when there is a
concrete, evidenced case that a named future signal will separate the two — an analyzer that is
specified, or a feature that is measurable and not yet measured. "A future analyzer might fix this"
is not sufficient. Record the evidence on the phenotype, or resolve the collision now.

Collision 1 is a live example: separating ghost from packshot needs a signal for *does the garment
hold a worn 3D shape* that PRISM does not currently measure. Whether such a signal is realistically
obtainable is the thing to establish — not assume.

**Depends on:** [[T-5030]] changes what `clipping-path` can mean, which changes collision 1. Sequence
accordingly.
